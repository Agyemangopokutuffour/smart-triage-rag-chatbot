HOSPITAL TRIAGE LEVELS AND SYMPTOM GUIDE
=========================================

WHAT IS TRIAGE?
Triage is the process of quickly assessing patients when they arrive at the hospital to determine how urgently they need medical care. Every patient is assigned a triage level based on the severity of their condition.

TRIAGE LEVEL 1 — IMMEDIATE (RED)
These patients are in life-threatening danger and need care right away.
Symptoms include:
- Chest pain or pressure (possible heart attack)
- Difficulty breathing or not breathing
- Unconsciousness or unresponsiveness
- Severe allergic reaction (throat swelling, cannot swallow)
- Uncontrolled or heavy bleeding
- Signs of stroke: face drooping on one side, arm weakness, slurred speech
- Severe head injury or trauma
- Suspected overdose from drugs or medication
- Seizures that do not stop
- Very low blood pressure (shock)
Expected wait time: Seen immediately. No waiting.

TRIAGE LEVEL 2 — URGENT (ORANGE)
These patients have serious conditions that could get worse quickly but are not immediately life-threatening.
Symptoms include:
- Chest pain without breathing difficulty (needs fast evaluation)
- High fever above 39.5°C (103°F) especially in children under 3 or adults over 65
- Severe abdominal pain
- Broken bones or suspected fractures
- Head injury where patient is conscious but confused
- Severe vomiting or diarrhea with signs of dehydration
- Moderate allergic reaction (hives, swelling but airway is fine)
- Mental health crisis (severe anxiety, suicidal thoughts)
- Sudden severe eye pain or vision loss
Expected wait time: Seen within 15 to 30 minutes.

TRIAGE LEVEL 3 — LESS URGENT (YELLOW)
These patients need medical care but their condition is stable and not worsening rapidly.
Symptoms include:
- Moderate pain (headache, back pain, tooth pain)
- Fever between 38°C and 39.5°C (100.4°F to 103°F)
- Minor cuts that may need stitches
- Mild to moderate breathing difficulty (asthma flare without severe distress)
- Urinary tract infection symptoms (burning, frequent urination)
- Mild vomiting or diarrhea without dehydration
- Sprain or strain injuries
- Ear pain or ear infection
- Eye redness or discharge (pink eye)
Expected wait time: Seen within 30 to 60 minutes.

TRIAGE LEVEL 4 — NON-URGENT (GREEN)
These patients have minor conditions that do not require immediate attention.
Symptoms include:
- Minor cuts, scrapes, or bruises
- Mild headache or cold symptoms
- Rash without other symptoms
- Mild sore throat
- Minor burns covering a small area
- Chronic condition follow-up
Expected wait time: Seen within 1 to 2 hours.

TRIAGE LEVEL 5 — MINOR (BLUE)
These patients have very minor issues that could often be handled at a pharmacy or GP clinic.
Symptoms include:
- Prescription refill requests
- Minor skin conditions
- General wellness questions
- Mild cold or flu without complications
Expected wait time: Seen within 2 hours or may be redirected to outpatient clinic.

IMPORTANT NOTE FOR PATIENTS:
The triage nurse will assess you when you arrive. Please be honest and thorough when describing your symptoms. Do not minimize your pain or discomfort — accurate information helps the team prioritize your care correctly. If your condition worsens while you are waiting, immediately inform the triage nurse or front desk staff.
